## The button
After the Americans with Disabilities Act (1990), lift doors must stay open long enough for people using wheelchairs or crutches, so on most US elevators the door-close button does nothing for regular riders ([The New York Times, via CBS Boston, 31 Oct 2016](https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/door-close-elevator-button-dont-work-placebo)). Door-open still works.

## Pressing 5 times
The controller stores requests in a **set**: floor 3 is either requested or not. Duplicates are ignored — the press is **idempotent**.

## Which floor first: SCAN
The lift keeps moving in its current direction and serves every request on the way, then reverses. This is the **SCAN / elevator algorithm** (also used for disk scheduling). It travels far less than serving requests in arrival order.

Example (illustration): lift at 3, requests arrive 5, 2, 4, 1, 6 → in arrival order it travels 15 floors; with SCAN (up 4, 5, 6, then down 2, 1) only 8.

## The interview answer: design an elevator (LLD)
- **States:** IDLE, MOVING_UP, MOVING_DOWN, DOORS_OPEN (+ MAINTENANCE).
- **Requests:** hall calls (floor + direction) and car calls (floor), stored as sets per direction.
- **Scheduler:** SCAN/LOOK per car; with many cars, a dispatcher assigns hall calls to the best car (nearest moving the right way).
- **Edge cases:** door timer, overload, emergency, fire mode.

**Words to say:** state machine, request set (idempotent), SCAN/LOOK, dispatcher.
