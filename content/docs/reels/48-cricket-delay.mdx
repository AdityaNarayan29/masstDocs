> **Ek line yaad rakho:** kam delay chahiye toh chhote tukde, par atakne ka risk.

## Where the delay comes from
1. **Segments:** live video is cut into chunks, often ~6 seconds long. A segment can only be sent once it's fully recorded.
2. **Encode → CDN → phone:** every hop adds time (plus playlist polling and CDN caching).
3. **Player buffer:** HLS players start **3 segments behind live** to avoid stalls — with 6 s segments that's **18 s** just in the buffer.

AWS notes OTT streams historically ran **40–60 seconds** behind live ([AWS Media blog, Low-Latency HLS explained](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/alhls-apple-low-latency-http-live-streaming-explained)). TV broadcast skips most of this, so your neighbour sees the six first.

## The fix: Low-Latency HLS
LL-HLS publishes **partial segments** (~0.33 s) while the 6 s segment is still being made, and holds playlist requests open until the next part is ready, so players can sit much closer to live.

## The interview answer: latency vs buffering
- Smaller segments/parts → **lower latency**, but less buffer → **more rebuffering** on bad networks.
- Bigger segments → smooth playback, more delay.
- Tune per use case: sports/betting want low latency; movies want smoothness.
- Mention: CDN edge caching, ABR (adaptive bitrate), LL-HLS / LL-DASH / WebRTC for sub-second.
