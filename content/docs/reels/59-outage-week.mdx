## The pattern
Google's SRE team: "roughly 70% of outages are due to changes in a live system" ([Tenets of SRE](https://oreilly.com/ideas/tenets-of-sre)). This week fits: upgrades, a new router, an internal update, a bug, maintenance.

## The interview answer: safe deployment + blast radius
- **Small changes, staged:** canary to a few users or one region, then widen.
- **Detect fast:** watch error rates and latency per stage; stop automatically.
- **Roll back in one step:** every change must be reversible; feature flags help.
- **Blast radius:** split the system into cells or compartments so one bad change only sinks one compartment.

**Words to say:** change management, canary, progressive rollout, rollback, feature flags, blast radius, cell-based architecture.

## Follow-up questions

**If changes cause most outages, why not change less?**
Freezing changes builds up risk: the next release is bigger and scarier. The goal is many small, safe, reversible changes.

**What would you add first to a team with no safe-deployment process?**
Automatic rollback on error-rate spikes, then staged rollouts by region, then cell isolation for the most critical service.
